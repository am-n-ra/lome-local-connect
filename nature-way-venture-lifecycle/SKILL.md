# Nature Way Venture Lifecycle

## Resource loading and attestation

Treat every named `references/` or `templates/` resource as a required input when its stated trigger applies. Load the exact file or directory before performing the dependent work, then include a **Resource Receipt** in the phase output: `Loaded`, `Template instantiated`, and `Not loaded / reason`. Do not claim to have followed a resource that was not loaded. If a required resource is unavailable, state the gap and stop before the gate it controls.

Use this skill to answer **“what must become true next for this venture to deserve its next level of commitment?”** It is a stage and evidence model, not a fixed calendar, valuation ladder, or pitch-deck recipe. A venture may move backward, pause, or skip a stage only when authoritative evidence makes the omitted question genuinely irrelevant.

## Intra-skill plan and task tree

Before substantive work inside this skill’s active gate, create or reconcile a local plan under the controlling Founder HQ Master Plan or specialist artifact. Use the structural path `HQ milestone > gate > specialist > slice > task > subtask`. Add a child only when it has its own objective, dependency, decision, state transition, failure mode, owner, or proof gate; stop at the smallest coherent unit that can be executed, reviewed, and proven without losing context.

Treat a **slice** as one end-to-end outcome or bounded evidence packet, a **task** as one coherent change or proof contribution with one owner, and a **subtask** as an atomic predecessor of that parent task. Keep dependencies, acceptance, expected proof, risk/debt boundary, owner, capacity impact, and re-plan trigger explicit. Use `todo → ready → in_progress → review → verified → done`, with `blocked`, `partial`, `manual`, or `deferred` when accurate. A child requires proof to close; a parent also requires its own acceptance and gate proof.

Re-plan when evidence contradicts an assumption, intent or a material decision changes, a dependency or capacity limit changes, a risk appears, or the gate condition fails. Preserve valid evidence, reopen only affected descendants, reconcile the controlling artifact first, and report added, removed, deferred, and reopened work. Every specialist return must include the active structural path, completed task/subtask IDs, proof IDs or links with an as-of time, residual gap or blocker, owner, next smallest action, capacity impact, re-plan trigger, and Resource Receipt. Read [references/intra-skill-planning-protocol.md](references/intra-skill-planning-protocol.md) when creating a nested plan, splitting a task, or preparing a handoff.

## Relationship to the Founder HQ ecosystem

Use **Nature Way Founder HQ** first when product, capital, opportunity, distribution, and learning work compete for attention. Use this skill to identify the current lifecycle stage and next venture-level proof. Route execution to **Nature Way**, capital readiness to **Nature Way Fundraising**, external pathways to **Opportunity Intelligence**, capability gaps to **Founder Learning**, and private reflection only to **Sidereal Reflection**.

## Lifecycle map

| Stage | Question to answer | Minimum evidence gate | Primary handoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Orientation | Which founder, customer context, and venture boundary are we exploring? | Explicit segment, problem space, non-goals, and decision owner. | Founder HQ and Nature Way Seed. |
| 1. Problem evidence | Is a specific group experiencing a meaningful, reachable problem? | Repeated observed behaviour, current workaround, urgency, and disconfirming evidence. | Customer discovery and Nature Way Seed. |
| 2. Solution hypothesis | Does a proposed outcome address the problem better than the current alternative? | Testable promise, riskiest assumption, prototype/demonstration plan, and failure criterion. | Nature Way Species/Root. |
| 3. Demo or prototype | Can the intended experience be understood, used, and technically delivered at the required depth? | Representative user journey, honest data boundary, feasibility and failure/recovery plan. | Nature Way Trunk. |
| 4. Customer value proof | Will target users activate, return, pay, commit time, or otherwise take a meaningful action? | Defined value event, evidence period, cohort/segment basis, and alternative explanation check. | Nature Way proof ledger. |
| 5. Distribution proof | Can the venture reach, convert, and support the target segment through a viable repeatable path? | Channel thesis, acquisition/activation experiment, cost/capacity basis, and learning loop. | Distribution experiments and product operations. |
| 6. Retention and economics | Does value repeat while delivery economics and reliability remain credible? | Retention/repeat evidence, unit/economic definitions, service constraints, and known limits. | Nature Way Canopy / Fundraising proof. |
| 7. Scale and financing readiness | What resource unlocks the next measurable milestone without weakening the core? | Default-alive plan, milestone model, capacity plan, capital thesis or non-dilutive alternative. | Nature Way Fundraising / Opportunity Intelligence. |

## Run stages as falsifiable loops

At the current stage, write one testable statement, the signal that would support it, the signal that would weaken it, the smallest ethical/reversible experiment, and the decision that follows. Separate interest from behaviour, stated intent from commitment, reach from activation, and activation from retention.

Do not treat a prototype, social attention, waitlist, signed but unpaid MOU, accelerator acceptance, or investor conversation as proof of a customer or distribution stage unless the defined stage evidence supports the claim.

## Evidence freshness

Set a review trigger for each stage conclusion. Reopen the stage when the target segment, customer workflow, offer, channel, price, product boundary, or evidence period materially changes. Older evidence may remain useful context, but it does not automatically validate a changed hypothesis.

Read [references/lifecycle-scorecard.md](references/lifecycle-scorecard.md) before assessing a venture stage or preparing a stage transition.

## Distribution is a core gate

Distribution is not a marketing task postponed until after product completion. At every stage, state who the reachable target segment is, where they already pay attention or transact, the offer/message being tested, the path from discovery to value event, the operational cost of service, and the signal that the path might repeat.

Start with a narrow **distribution thesis**. Test one plausible route at a time where practical: direct outreach, customer referral, community, content, integration, partner/channel, sales, procurement, marketplace, or another context-appropriate path. Preserve channel, audience, message, offer, date, cost/capacity, result, and what changed. Do not claim repeatability from a one-off founder-led effort without recording why it might generalise.

Read [references/distribution-experiments.md](references/distribution-experiments.md) before planning a channel test, growth experiment, launch acquisition plan, or scale claim.

## Financing stage boundaries

Use labels such as pre-seed, seed, and Series A only as external market shorthand. Do not let a named round determine the evidence needed. Capital is appropriate only when its amount, instrument, obligations, and timing are connected to the next measurable venture milestone and a default-alive plan remains viable if it does not close. Use Nature Way Fundraising for capital-fit, investor process, claims, and counsel boundaries.

## Required artifacts

| Artifact | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Lifecycle thesis | Segment, problem space, current stage, target next proof, non-goals, and owner. |
| Stage scorecard | Evidence, counter-evidence, confidence, limitation, gate decision, and review date. |
| Assumption / experiment register | Test statement, smallest experiment, signal, result, decision, and next action. |
| Distribution thesis and experiment log | Audience, channel, message/offer, path, cost/capacity, outcome, and learning. |
| Milestone map | Current proof, next venture proof, resource need, constraints, and handoffs. |

## Definition of done

Call a lifecycle review complete only when the current stage is named without exaggeration; its evidence and counter-evidence are recorded; one next stage gate is explicit; distribution has been considered rather than deferred; product, capital, opportunity, and learning handoffs are current; and the Founder HQ Board contains one owned next action. Never imply that a stage, channel, round, or framework guarantees venture success.
