# Founder intent discovery

Use this reference before a new product, a major feature, or a restart of undocumented work. Help the founder clarify the decision; do not demand a technical specification.

## Run short discovery loops

Ask one to three high-value questions, reflect the answer in plain language, and identify what changed. Prefer observed context and current workarounds to imagined personas or feature lists. Do not ask questions whose answers will not affect the next gate.

| Loop | Clarify | Questions to adapt |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Why this matters now. | What happened? Who experiences the friction? What makes it costly or urgent? |
| Current reality | Existing alternatives and evidence. | What is done today instead? Where does that fail? What evidence do we have versus assume? |
| Intended outcome | The change worth creating. | What becomes easier, safer, faster, or more reliable? For whom? What must not get worse? |
| Boundary | Scope and constraints. | What is not being built now? What budget, time, device, data, or operational limits apply? |
| First proof | The smallest learning journey. | What is the smallest actor-to-outcome path that would change our confidence? What would weaken the hypothesis? |

## Separate four things

Write the **need**, **proposed solution**, **feature ideas**, and **assumptions** separately. Do not let a named feature become the problem statement. Where the founder has several plausible objectives, offer a small set of trade-offs and ask which one owns the current milestone.

## Close the Seed

Close only when the founder can correct or confirm an Intent Brief containing: problem, actors, context, desired outcome, alternatives, critical journey, success signal, harm to avoid, constraints, non-goals, hypotheses, risk level, and next proof. If a missing question could materially change the chosen journey or architecture, keep it open and do not enter Species or Root.

## Existing products

For an existing repository, reconstruct the Intent Brief from documents, code, analytics, issues, and user feedback where available. Label every inferred item. Ask the founder to confirm or correct the intent before treating legacy behavior as a valid product requirement.
