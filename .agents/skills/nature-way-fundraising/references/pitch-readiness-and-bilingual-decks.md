# Pitch Readiness and Bilingual Deck Reference

Use this reference when a fundraising, investor, accelerator, or demo-day deck must be bilingual in English and French. Build an original deck from the venture’s verified work. Do not recreate a source deck’s branding, proprietary copy, assets, layouts, or visual identity.

## Pitch-readiness gate

Do not begin visual deck production until the following inputs have a source owner, as-of date, and evidence status.

| Input | Required evidence | Common weak substitute to reject |
|---|---|---|
| Product positioning | Specific customer, pain, job, and differentiated value proposition. | Category label or vague mission. |
| Pricing and revenue logic | Price basis, discount rules, actual conversion or stated assumption. | A price slide with no customer or margin logic. |
| Business model | Who pays, for what, when, and how money flows. | A generic business-model canvas with no operating proof. |
| Financial assumptions | Owner-backed forecast assumptions, burn/runway basis, and key sensitivities. | Lines that only go up. |
| Customer acquisition | Channel, cost/time basis, pipeline/experiment evidence, and owner. | Downloads, followers, or unqualified leads. |
| Traction | Earned product use, paid contracts, renewal/retention, or explicitly bounded pilot evidence. | Signed but unpaid MOUs or vanity counts. |
| Go-to-market | A sequence of target segment, channel, sales motion, expansion trigger, and measurement. | A market list without a route to customer. |

If a required input is only a hypothesis, label it as such and specify the next validation event. Deck completion is not a substitute for work on the underlying venture.

## Use an original bilingual system

Select one bilingual mode deliberately. The default is **paired hierarchy**: English carries the primary title and core statement, while French follows immediately as an equivalent secondary reading layer. Use **paired slides** for dense content that cannot retain legibility in one frame. Use **separate decks** only when the audience, legal review, or local specificity requires different full documents.

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Claim source | Finalise the source-language claim and its evidence before translation. |
| Equivalence | Preserve figures, dates, metric definitions, caveats, obligations, and asks across languages. |
| Hierarchy | Give the primary language first position; give the second language sufficient size, contrast, and proximity to be independently readable. |
| Translation | Translate meaning and investor terminology, not word order. Use a reviewer fluent in both languages for material claims. |
| Visual system | Use an original palette, typography, iconography, grid, and component language that fit the venture; never copy a training deck’s logos or composition. |
| Accessibility | Do not rely on colour alone; keep text readable at presentation distance and preserve logical reading order for exported PDFs. |
| Consistency | Translate recurring labels, financial terms, and status words once in a terminology register and use them identically throughout. |

## Build the deck from actual work

Use the existing claim-to-source ledger as the deck’s source of truth. Typical slides may cover the customer problem, product/value proposition, proof and traction, market logic, business model, go-to-market, competition/defensibility, team, financial/milestone plan, and specific ask. Adapt the order to the investor and venture stage; do not add an unsupported slide merely to follow a template.

For every slide, record the claim IDs, figures, language mode, source owner, and the decision or question the slide must answer. Use plain bilingual labels for section headers, cards, and charts. Keep a clear distinction between actuals, forecasts, targets, and hypotheses.

## Run structured mock pitches

Before a live pitch, rehearse with a panel that can challenge the business rather than merely review visual design. Score the presentation using the following dimensions: product-market fit, commercial viability, business model, scalability, execution capability, and storytelling. Record feedback as a change request with an evidence status; do not “polish” around a substantive objection.

| Dimension | Question | Evidence / change required |
|---|---|---|
| Product-market fit | Does the customer pain and current proof make the solution necessary now? | `<claim IDs, customer evidence, gap>` |
| Commercial viability | Is there a credible path to paying, retained customers? | `<price, sales/retention evidence, gap>` |
| Business model | Does the revenue logic fit the customer and cost base? | `<unit-economics basis, gap>` |
| Scalability | Is the expansion path operationally and economically credible? | `<market ladder, capacity or channel evidence>` |
| Execution capability | Can this team deliver the next financed milestone? | `<team, operating record, ownership>` |
| Storytelling | Can an informed stranger repeat the problem, proof, ask, and milestone accurately? | `<rehearsal feedback and revision>` |

End each rehearsal with a decision: `ready`, `revise narrative`, `produce evidence`, `fix operations`, or `defer raise`. Retest material revisions before declaring the deck pitch-ready.
