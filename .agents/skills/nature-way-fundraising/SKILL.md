# Nature Way Fundraising

## Resource loading and attestation

Treat every named `references/` or `templates/` resource as a required input when its stated trigger applies. Load the exact file or directory before performing the dependent work, then include a **Resource Receipt** in the phase output: `Loaded`, `Template instantiated`, and `Not loaded / reason`. Do not claim to have followed a resource that was not loaded. If a required resource is unavailable, state the gap and stop before the gate it controls.

For every active fundraising gate, load [references/intra-skill-execution-controller.md](references/intra-skill-execution-controller.md) and instantiate [templates/intra-skill-plan.md](templates/intra-skill-plan.md). Create the local dependency-aware task tree before readiness, narrative, diligence, or outreach work, and return its evidence, blockers, and task changes to Founder HQ.

## Founder HQ integration

When fundraising competes with product delivery, opportunity work, or founder capacity, use **Nature Way Founder HQ** to reconcile the active milestone and capacity cost. Nature Way Fundraising remains the authority for capital claims, data-room readiness, investor process, commitments, and post-close reporting.

Treat fundraising as a growth ring around a real business, not as a deck-generation exercise. The quality of the raise depends on evidence that the product, market, team, records, and governance have already produced. A polished deck can open a conversation; it cannot replace proof, retention, clean records, a recoverable data room, or capital that fits the company.

## Relationship to Nature Way development

Use **Nature Way** to grow a product through Seed, Species, Root System, Trunk, Heartwood, Branches, Canopy, and Rings. Use this skill when the company needs to turn the resulting evidence into a fundable operating system.

Do not start a raise merely because a prototype exists. First identify the product’s verified trunk, the current proof gaps, and the capital type that matches the business. Do not treat investment readiness as a guarantee of financing, valuation, or investor interest.

## The fundraising growth cycle

| Phase | Objective | Required output | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Seed — Capital thesis | Decide whether capital is needed, what milestone it unlocks, and which capital types fit. | Raise thesis, use-of-funds logic, alternatives and non-goals. | Capital is tied to a measurable milestone, not a generic wish list. |
| 1. Species — Investor narrative | Define an honest, evidence-led narrative for the right investor audience. | Narrative spine, metric dictionary, deck/memo outline, bilingual system where needed, and disclosure rules. | Every headline maps to a source or is labelled as a hypothesis. |
| 2. Root System — Readiness | Establish legal, financial, product, market, team, governance and documentation foundations. | Readiness score, remediation plan, ownership map and data-room index. | A stranger can verify the claimed evidence within the agreed response window. |
| 3. Trunk — Proof | Turn current operating evidence into a specific investment case. | Traction/retention/cohort evidence, bottom-up market logic, unit-economics basis and milestone plan. | The business case works without unsupported superlatives or vanity metrics. |
| 4. Heartwood — Diligence durability | Prepare claims to withstand scrutiny before a live process. | Data-room completeness check, claim-to-source ledger, cap-table/obligation review and risk log. | Every material claim has a source, owner, date and caveat. |
| 5. Branches — Capital fit and relationships | Target matched capital sources and create durable relationships. | Capital-fit map, investor pipeline, relationship routes and contact cadence. | Each target has an explicit stage, sector, geography, cheque, rationale and next action. |
| 6. Canopy — Active raise | Run a controlled, respectful, evidence-led process. | Meeting plan, follow-up log, diligence tracker, process calendar and decision log. | The company can answer requests without losing control of information, timing or truth. |
| 7. Rings — Close and compound | Close carefully, then convert the relationship into accountable governance. | Closing checklist, post-close plan, investor-update rhythm and next-round proof plan. | Terms, conditions, ownership, funds receipt and reporting responsibilities are documented. |

## Phase 0 — Write the capital thesis

State the business stage, current verified evidence, milestone to be purchased, amount and duration logic, capital constraints, and alternatives such as revenue, grants, partnerships, debt, or non-dilutive support. Do not assume venture equity is the right answer.

Use capital only where the business can state what it unlocks and how the resulting milestone will be measured. If the company cannot plausibly fit a fund-returning outcome, do not force a venture narrative; frame the appropriate financing path instead.

Maintain a default-alive operating plan alongside the raise: state the work, spend, commitments and customer obligations that continue if capital does not close by the planning date. Do not make operating commitments solely on expected capital before closing conditions and funds receipt justify the change.

Read [references/capital-fit-and-pipeline.md](references/capital-fit-and-pipeline.md) before selecting capital targets.

## Phase 1–2 — Produce a verifiable narrative and readiness base

Build the narrative from evidence rather than adjectives. Separate facts, calculations, management forecasts, hypotheses, and requested capital. Define each metric, time period, currency and source. Preserve bad news with context; do not remove it by changing the label.

Score the seven readiness pillars on an evidence basis, then attack the weakest material pillars first. A score is a diagnostic, not a funding prediction. Build the data room before the process, not after an investor asks.

Read [references/readiness-and-data-room.md](references/readiness-and-data-room.md) before creating a scorecard, a data room, or a readiness remediation plan.

## Pitch-readiness and bilingual deck gate

Treat a pitch deck as a structured view of work already done: product positioning, pricing, business model, financial assumptions, customer acquisition, traction evidence, practical go-to-market, and the specific capital milestone. Do not use a deck to paper over missing proof.

When the audience requires English and French, choose a deliberate bilingual system. Default to a paired hierarchy in which English carries the primary title and core claim while French immediately provides an equivalent secondary reading layer. Keep both languages independently readable. Translate final, sourced claims rather than translating draft prose; preserve all figures, dates, definitions, caveats, and asks exactly across languages. Use original visual direction—never copy another training deck’s branding, logos, assets, or layout.

Run structured mock pitches before live investor use. Test product-market fit, commercial viability, business model, scalability, execution capability, and storytelling. Convert each material criticism into an evidence, operating, narrative, or design change with an owner; retest after revision.

Read [references/pitch-readiness-and-bilingual-decks.md](references/pitch-readiness-and-bilingual-decks.md) before drafting an investor deck, accelerator presentation, bilingual pitch, or mock-pitch rubric.

## Phase 3–4 — Prove the investment case and its durability

Use bottom-up market sizing, earned traction, cohort/retention evidence, unit-economics definitions, risk disclosures and a milestone plan. Do not substitute app downloads, signed but unpaid MOUs, gross transaction volume, social followers or a total-addressable-market headline for commercial proof.

Create a claim-to-source ledger before drafting the deck. Every repeated financial or operational figure must reconcile across the deck, memo, data room and model. Preserve dates and accounting basis. Do not fabricate forecasts, customer results, contracts, usage, pipeline, investor interest, or expected outcomes.

## Product-to-capital proof handoff

Carry Nature Way product proof into fundraising through a capital-proof packet rather than by rewriting it as narrative. Map each investor-facing claim to its product proof ID, evidence class, source/as-of date, metric definition, limitation, confidentiality tier, and owner. Use unqualified claims only where evidence has been reproduced or externally confirmed. Label bounded, manual, and unproven evidence honestly.

If a product change alters a metric, price, availability, permission model, data authority, launch scope, reliability state, or operating owner, update the capital-proof packet, claim ledger, data room, deck, and investor update before the next external use. If a fundraising commitment changes product priorities, return to Nature Way Seed or Root System before building against it.

Read [references/evidence-and-claim-ledger.md](references/evidence-and-claim-ledger.md) when preparing the narrative, deck, memo, KPI set, or model assumptions.

Read [references/product-proof-handoff.md](references/product-proof-handoff.md) whenever a product outcome, demo, pilot, metric, launch, or roadmap item appears in fundraising material.

## External-pack version control

Before every investor-facing meeting, disclosure, or data-room share, record the exact deck/memo/model/data-room version, as-of date, owner, and material changes since the prior external use. If a source metric or product claim changes, update or retire the affected external file rather than allowing several plausible-but-inconsistent versions to circulate.

## Phase 5–7 — Run the relationship and closing process

Target matched investors or capital sources rather than broadcasting indiscriminately. Match by stage, mandate, geography, sector, cheque size, decision speed, capital type, portfolio conflict and genuine relationship route. Build relationships before a high-pressure ask where possible. Keep a complete record of interactions, follow-ups, requests, confidentiality and commitments.

During diligence, answer accurately and quickly, but do not disclose confidential customer, employee or partner information without authority. Escalate term-sheet, tax, securities, corporate, employment and regulatory matters to qualified local counsel and finance professionals. Do not tell a user which term, valuation, investor or capital instrument to accept; support the evidence, comparison and decision process instead.

Read [references/raise-process-and-governance.md](references/raise-process-and-governance.md) before a live raise, diligence, term-sheet process or post-close reporting design.

Read [references/raise-contingency-and-operating-plan.md](references/raise-contingency-and-operating-plan.md) before starting or extending a raise, changing the operating plan around an expected close, or allocating founder time between company execution and fundraising.

## Operating rules

| Rule | Required behaviour |
|---|---|
| Evidence before outreach | Do not start broad outreach while material readiness gaps remain unowned. |
| Claim provenance | Give every material claim a source, as-of date, owner and status. |
| Capital fit over prestige | Prefer capital whose economics, timeline and obligations fit the business stage. |
| Targeted process | Use a documented investor universe and relationship route; do not mass-email indiscriminately. |
| Truthful uncertainty | Label forecasts, estimates, ranges, missing evidence and risks. Never invent precision. |
| Data-room discipline | Make documents findable, permissioned, current and responsive before diligence. |
| Original bilingual delivery | Use source-controlled bilingual claims and original visual language; never copy a training deck or hide one language below readable hierarchy. |
| Product-proof continuity | Preserve the product proof class, date, metric basis and limitation in every fundraising claim. |
| Confidentiality ladder | Use an approved disclosure tier for each item; do not expose customer, employee, product, or contractual details for process speed. |
| Learning loop | Record material declines, diligence gaps, and pitch feedback as evidence, narrative, product, or capital-fit actions before the next cycle. |
| Default-alive plan | Keep operations and commitments viable without assuming an unclosed raise; revisit when runway, obligations, or process conditions change. |
| Counsel boundary | Seek qualified professional advice for terms, securities, tax, employment and legal obligations. |
| Compounding relationship | Send regular, candid updates after the raise as well as before it. |

## Definition of done for a fundraising ring

Call a ring ready only when the capital thesis is tied to a measurable milestone; the default-alive plan remains viable without an assumed close; material claims can be verified through the capital-proof packet; any investor deck reflects actual work and has passed its mock-pitch gate; bilingual claims are cross-language consistent where required; readiness gaps have owners; the data room is current and permissioned; capital targets are matched rather than aspirational; risks are candidly disclosed; disclosures follow approved confidentiality tiers; legal and financial professional decisions are escalated appropriately; and a post-close reporting rhythm exists. Never call a company “fundable,” “investment-ready,” or “guaranteed to raise” solely because its deck reads well.
